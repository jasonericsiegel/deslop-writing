# Drafting Rules

*Drafting rules version: 1.1*

How to write the draft in the first place, so less of it needs repairing afterwards.

These are moves for producing prose, not tests for finding faults in it. Everything here is a thing to do while the sentence is still forming. Nothing here is a list of words to avoid. That is a choice about this file's job: catching named tells belongs to the editing pass. A short list of specific patterns does no harm before writing; a long or vague one ('don't sound like AI') tends to swap one habit for another.

Two things govern the rest. **Say what is true**: no rule below is worth a claim you did not mean, and where a rule and the meaning pull apart, the meaning wins. **Sound like yourself**: the goal is prose that reads as one specific person thinking, not prose scrubbed until nobody is home. Write clumsily rather than write something false or something flat.

## Voice and sentences

- **Say it the way you would say it out loud** to one smart person who already respects you. If a sentence would sound strange spoken, write the spoken version instead.
- **One idea per sentence.** When a second idea arrives, start a new sentence.
- **Reach for the shortest word that carries the exact meaning.** *Use*, not *utilize*. *Help*, not *facilitate*. *Because*, not *due to the fact that*.
- **Keep the specific word when a plainer one would change the claim.** Plainness serves accuracy and never outranks it. If the simpler word makes a different claim, the specific word is the right word.
- **Name who does the thing.** Write *the compiler rejects it*, not *it is rejected*. Reach for a subject that acts.
- **Pick a verb that already carries the force**, so you do not need an adverb to prop it up. *Sprinted*, not *ran quickly*.
- **Use contractions.** *It's*, *don't*, *we've*. That is how people talk, and it is the plainer form.
- **Put a period between two complete thoughts.** A comma, a colon before a payoff, or parentheses around an aside will handle almost everything else. Save the em-dash for a break sharp enough that no other mark does the job, and use at most one in a paragraph.
- **Give a number, a name, or an example** wherever you would otherwise write an abstraction.

## Shape

The rules above make sentences plainer. These decide what the piece is. Nobody cleaning up the wording later can make these choices for you, because by then the shape is already set.

- **Open with the point.** The first sentence carries the claim. Whatever the reader needs in order to parse that claim comes second, and only as much of it as they need.
- **Lead each paragraph with its claim**, then support it. One claim per paragraph.
- **Stop at the last real point.** When the final piece of substance is on the page, the piece is finished. A closing paragraph that restates what the reader just read is a second copy, not an ending.
- **End on the last concrete thing you said.** A closing line earns its place by reusing something already on the page: a number, a name, a fact the reader has met. One that would sit equally well at the end of someone else's piece has not earned it, however good it sounds.
- **Order sections by what the reader needs next**, not by the order in which you worked it out.
- **Earn every sentence as you write it.** If you cannot say what a sentence is doing that the sentence before it did not, leave it out rather than writing it and trimming later.
- **Write headings in sentence case, and make them say something.** A heading that names the finding beats one that names the topic. Follow it with content, not a line restating it.
- **Choose prose unless the content is genuinely a list or a comparison.** Related ideas need connective tissue, which bullets strip out.

## The rule about the rules

Applied at full strength to every sentence, any list like this produces flat, even prose, which is the thing it exists to prevent. These are defaults for when nothing better suggests itself. Break any of them sooner than write something clumsy.
